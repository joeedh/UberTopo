## 1. Split: P4a now, P4b next

P4 as listed is two phases of work. Like P3, it is split.

**P4a (this plan): exact curves as edge carriers.**
- A `curves/` library of exact, certified 2D and 3D curves.
- Certified polynomial approximation (quintic Hermite B-splines) and flattening.
- The geometry store in `Complex`, following the pool owner contract.
- Edge carriers (`.e.curve` + `.e.range`) in the editor, the validator and I/O.
- Deterministic transcendental functions in litestl.

**P4b (later plan): surfaces and the road acceptance case.**
- Surfaces: an explicit `Plane`, `RoadSurface(s, t)`, `Heightfield`.
- Pcurves on coedges and on edges embedded in faces.
- Cross-sections, superelevation and gravity-referenced frames.
- Projection, closest point and intersection queries.
- Curved-geometry validation that replaces the chord skeleton (OI-P4a-1).
- Builder support, Bertolazzi–Frego G1 fitting, non-polynomial κ laws, septic (G3)
  approximation.
- The acceptance case: a road imprinted on terrain sheets.

P4a's acceptance: certified-bound tests and oracle comparison for every P4a kind, and
curved edges that survive the full cycle of edits, undo/redo, barriers, freeze and I/O.

## 2. Decisions (proposed DEC-018)

**Library and numerics:**
- **Library.** `source/ubertopo/curves/`, namespace `ubertopo::curves`. Evaluation is
  in `double`, in the tile frame's local coordinates (the same frame-local doubles as
  `geom::to_double3`). Nothing here is templated on the coordinate type (05 §2).
- **Deterministic transcendentals (owner decision).** Curve code never calls `std::`
  sin, cos, exp, cosh, sinh or asinh, because design 04 §8 forbids them on decision
  paths.
  - litestl gains `math/detmath.h`, with range reduction and fixed polynomials and no
    FMA. It gives the same bits under clang, gcc and MSVC, and has a stated error
    bound in ulps, which feeds `eval_error`.
- **κ laws are polynomials of degree ≤ 3 in P4a.** This covers lines, arcs,
  clothoids, cubic spirals and Bloss transitions. θ(s) is then a polynomial, so every
  bound below is polynomial algebra. Other laws are P4b (OI-P4a-3).

**Certification:**
- **What every kind reports:**
  - `eval_error`: a bound on the distance between the computed and the exact point of
    its definition;
  - `deriv_eval_error(k)`: the same bound for the k-th derivative;
  - `deriv_bound(t0, t1, k)`: an upper bound on max |r⁽ᵏ⁾| over a range.

  Box bounds, flattening and Hermite approximation are generic algorithms over these.
- **What "certified" means:**
  - proved for exact arithmetic;
  - plus an explicit rounding allowance for every floating-point step, bounds
    included: every computed bound is rounded outward through `up()`;
  - checked against a quad-precision oracle.
- **G2 by default.** The approximation is a quintic Hermite B-spline in the source
  parameter. Knot multiplicity follows the source's continuity at each special
  parameter (§3.7).

**Store and editing:**
- **The store.** `GeomRef` becomes `attr::PoolRef` (8 bytes). The geometry store is a
  `PoolStore` held by value in `Complex`, with kinds registered in a fixed order.
- **The editor never renumbers the live store.** Barrier undo and rollback restore
  the groups and keep the live store (§5.2). Only freeze, which is an outside write,
  canonicalises the live store.
- **Collects are explicit:** barriers (after they commit) and
  `Editor::collect_geometry()` collect; a collect is refused inside a transaction.
  Nothing collects on a threshold (OI-P4a-2).
- **One hash path.** `content_hash` hashes the groups as if canonicalised: refs are
  translated through a read-only canonical map (litestl), with no copy. It also
  includes the canonical store hash, whose closure is reachable refs only.
  Unreferenced entries never change a hash, so a failed edit stays bit-identical.
- **Endpoint tolerance.** An edge's carrier must meet its end vertices within
  `eval_error + √3·rounding_C(p) + curve_tol + rounding of the distance computation`.
  - `rounding_C`: per axis, half the quantum for `Lattice` and ½ ulp for `double` and
    `float`.
  - `curve_tol`: the global `.g.curve_tol`, Topo, default 2⁻³⁰ frame units.
- **Chord skeleton.** The geometric validator keeps checking straight chords between
  vertices. A curved edge's carrier is checked for kind, range and endpoints
  (OI-P4a-1).

**Format:**
- **UTCX format 3** stores the geometry store.
- **Formats 1 and 2 still read (owner decision)**, through legacy schemas in
  `io_legacy.cc`, against fixture bytes captured before the switch.
- The goldens are re-pinned with the proof in §5.4.

## 3. The curves library

### 3.1 Polynomials and piecewise functions (`curves/poly.h`, `curves/fn1d.h`)

**`Poly`:**
- Up to degree 63, with `double` coefficients.
- Operations: evaluate (Horner), differentiate, integrate, multiply, shift and scale
  the variable.
- Each operation has a rounding bound.
- **Range bound** over an interval:
  1. map it to [0, 1];
  2. convert to Bernstein form, with a rounding bound;
  3. take the coefficients' extremes, rounded outward.

  The convex hull property makes the result certified.
- **Real roots in an interval:** isolate by Bernstein sign changes and bisection, then
  refine with a fixed number of safeguarded Newton steps. Roots at the interval ends
  and double roots are reported with a multiplicity.

**`Fn1D`:** a piecewise polynomial over breakpoints s₀ < s₁ < …
- **Coefficients are local** to each piece (σ = s − s_i).
- **Canonical form:**
  - trailing zero coefficients are trimmed;
  - −0.0 → +0.0;
  - zero-length pieces are dropped;
  - non-finite values are refused.

  Pieces are never merged: merging would round the shifted coefficients. Two functions
  built the same way therefore intern to one entry, while the same function split
  differently does not.
- **Uses:** lane widths w(s) and vertical profiles z(s).

### 3.2 The kind interface (`curves/curve.h`)

```cpp
enum class ParamKind : uint8_t { ArcLength, Station, General };
enum class Side : uint8_t { Below, Above };          // one-sided limits at special params
struct SpecialParam { double t; uint8_t continuity; }; // C^continuity at t (0, 1, 2, 3 = smoother)

template <int D> class CurveEval {            // D = 2 or 3
public:
  virtual Interval domain() const = 0;
  virtual ParamKind param_kind() const = 0;
  /* r, r', ..., r^(n) at t, n <= 6; side picks the limit at a special param */
  virtual void eval(double t, int n, Vec<D> *out, Side side = Side::Above) const = 0;
  virtual double eval_error() const = 0;              // |computed r - exact r|, whole domain
  virtual double deriv_eval_error(int k) const = 0;   // same for r^(k), k <= 2
  /* max over [t0, t1] of |r^(k)|, k = 1..6, on a range with no special param inside */
  virtual double deriv_bound(double t0, double t1, int k) const = 0;
  virtual void special_params(double t0, double t1, Vector<SpecialParam> &out) const = 0;
};
```

- **Dispatch is virtual, per kind** (05 §2): there is no switch over kinds.
  - A kind registers a `KindOps` object that builds a `CurveEval` from a store entry.
  - The pool ID selects the `KindOps` from a table filled at registration.
- **A `CurveView`** (store, ref) → evaluator is valid until the next `intern`,
  `collect` or `canonicalize` on that store.
- **Inner refs are resolved when the view is built**, for example `alignment3`'s
  horizontal curve.
- **Every constructor validates its input.** It refuses NaN and inf, lengths ≤ 0,
  a ≤ 0, unsorted knots, and piece counts above `kMaxPieces` (2²⁰).

### 3.3 `cesaro2`: planar curvature-law curves

**Definition:**
- a start pose (x₀, y₀, θ₀);
- segments `{L_i, κ_i(σ)}`, where κ_i is a polynomial of degree ≤ 3 in the local arc
  length σ ∈ [0, L_i].

Segments chain: each starts at the previous segment's end pose. Heading is continuous,
and κ may jump at a join. The parameter is the arc length s ∈ [0, ΣL_i].

**Canonical form:**
- κ coefficients go through `Fn1D`'s rules;
- zero-length segments are dropped;
- −0.0 → +0.0;
- θ₀ is reduced to (−π, π] with `detmath`.

Equal definitions produce bitwise-equal entries and intern to one entry. Hashing,
equality and I/O cover the definition only. The derived piece table is rebuilt by
`read`.

**Derived pieces.** The piece table is built at construction:
1. Each segment's θ is a polynomial: θ₀ plus the exact integral of κ.
2. Each piece of half-length r = h/2 is expanded around its midpoint m:
   Q(u) = θ(m + u) − θ(m) = Σ_{j=1}^{4} q_j u^j.
3. **Bend first.** A piece is bisected until Q̂(r) = Σ_j |q_j| r^j ≤ 0.5, before any
   exponential is evaluated. Then Q̂(R) ≤ 4⁴·0.5 = 128 for R = 4r, so the exponential
   cannot overflow.
4. The integrand e^{iQ(u)} = Σ e_k u^k is expanded to order N = 30, by the recurrence
   e_{k+1} = (i/(k+1)) Σ_j (j+1) q_{j+1} e_{k−j}. Integrating term by term gives the
   position polynomial.
5. **Certified error per piece** is the sum of:
   - **Taylor tail.** The series of e^{Q̂} majorises e^{iQ}. Its coefficients ê_k are
     computed up to K = 160, and the tail is at most
     Σ_{N<k≤K} ê_k r^k + e^{Q̂(R)}·(r/R)^{K+1}/(1 − r/R). The second term is at most
     e¹²⁸·4⁻¹⁶¹·4/3 ≈ 2⁻¹³⁷. The tail of the integral is 2r times that.
   - **Rounding of the q_j:** from the midpoint shift, bounded through `Poly`'s
     rounding bounds and propagated as |Δq_j|·r^j·e^{Q̂(r)}·2r.
   - **Rounding of the recurrence:** at most γ_{cN}·Σ ê_k r^{k+1}, by the majorant.
   - **Horner rounding:** γ₆₂·Σ ê_k r^{k+1}.
   - **Rotation:** the `detmath` sincos error bound times the chord.
6. **Bisect** further until the certified error is at most 2⁻⁵⁰·h. Bisection is a
   deterministic function of the definition.
7. Piece start points chain with compensated summation. Each piece stores its start
   point, θ(m), the 31 coefficients and its error budget.

**Evaluation:**
- **`eval(s)`:** binary search for the piece, then Horner, then rotation by e^{iθ(m)}.
- **Derivatives** come from θ directly: r' = e^{iθ}, and r⁽ᵏ⁾ = P_{k−1}(s)·e^{iθ},
  with P_0 = 1 and P_{j+1} = P_j' + iκ·P_j.
- **`eval_error`:** the sum of the piece budgets (start-point errors accumulate), plus
  the heading rounding × length.
- **`deriv_eval_error(k)`:** the rounding of the P_{k−1} evaluation plus sincos.

**Bounds:**
- **`deriv_bound(k)`:** |r⁽ᵏ⁾| = |P_{k−1}|, which has degree ≤ 3(k − 2) ≤ 12 for
  k ≤ 6. It is bounded by the Bernstein bounds of Re and Im on the segments in range,
  combined as √(re² + im²) and rounded up.
- **Special parameters:**
  - segment joins, with continuity C1 where κ jumps, C2 where κ' jumps, and C3 where
    only higher derivatives jump;
  - inflections (roots of κ) and curvature extrema (roots of κ'), both C∞.

### 3.4 `offset2`: lateral offsets of a reference line

- **Definition:** base = a `PoolRef` to a `cesaro2`, plus w = an `Fn1D` over the
  base's station. It has inner refs.
- **Meaning.** The point is the base point moved by w(s) along the **base's** normal:
  r_w(s) = r(s) + w(s)·N(s), with N = i·e^{iθ}. This is road semantics: every lane edge
  is measured from the reference line.
  - It is not the geometric offset of another offset curve. Two lane widths therefore
    compose by adding w, because both are measured from the same base.
  - There is no offset-of-an-offset kind. The base must be a `cesaro2`, and the API
    takes `(cesaro2, w₁ + w₂)`.
  - A zero w collapses to the base.
- **Evaluation:**
  - from the exact base, never from an approximation (05 §4);
  - r_w' = Q_0·e^{iθ}, with Q_0 = 1 − wκ + i·w′, and higher derivatives by
    Q_{j+1} = Q_j' + iκ·Q_j;
  - the parameter is the base station (`ParamKind::Station`).
- **Special parameters:**
  - the base's;
  - w's breakpoints, at the continuity of w there;
  - folds: the roots of 1 − wκ, found by exact polynomial roots.

  `offset_valid(t0, t1)` reports whether 1 − wκ > 0 throughout.
- **Bounds:** `eval_error` = base error + |w| × the normal's rounding + the rounding of
  the w·N product.

### 3.5 `alignment3`: horizontal × vertical profile

- **Definition:**
  - horizontal = a `PoolRef` to a `cesaro2` or `offset2`;
  - profile z = an `Fn1D` over the horizontal's parameter.

  The parameter is the horizontal station (`Station`).
- **Evaluation:** r(s) = (h(s), z(s)).
- **Bounds:**
  - derivatives: √(bound_h² + bound_z²);
  - special parameters: the union of the horizontal's and the profile's, at the lower
    continuity.
- **Role:** the 3D carrier kind for road and rail centrelines and lane edges. Cant and
  superelevation belong to P4b's `RoadSurface`.

### 3.6 `catenary3`, `polyline3`, `bspline`

**`catenary3`:**
- **Definition:** anchors A and B, plus a > 0. The curve lies in the vertical plane
  through A and B: r(x) = A + x·û + (f(x) − f(0))·ẑ, with f(x) = a·cosh((x − c)/a)
  and x ∈ [0, d].
- c is derived (via `detmath::asinh`) so that the curve passes through B.
- **Evaluation:** f(x) − f(0) = 2a·sinh((x − 2c)/(2a))·sinh(x/(2a)). This form has no
  cancellation when a ≫ d.
- `from_sag(A, B, sag)` solves for a with a fixed number of Newton steps.
- **Bounds:**
  - derivatives: a^{1−k}·cosh or sinh, bounded by their values at the range ends
    (cosh and |sinh| are monotone away from c, which is a special parameter);
  - `eval_error` is absolute: about a·ulp, plus the detmath bounds.

**`polyline3`:**
- **Definition:** points; the parameter is the index, t ∈ [0, n − 1].
- Every vertex is a special parameter with continuity C0.
- deriv_bound(k ≥ 2) = 0 inside a span.
- `eval_error` is the rounding of the linear interpolation.

**`bspline`:**
- **Definition:**
  - dimension 2 or 3;
  - degree ≤ 7, with clamped, sorted knots and control points; non-rational in P4a;
  - optionally a source `PoolRef`, a source range and `max_error`, which make it an
    approximation cache. It has inner refs.
- **Evaluation:** de Boor; derivatives through derivative control points.
- **Bounds:** deriv_bound converts the spans in range to Bézier form (by knot
  insertion) and takes the Bernstein bound of the k-th derivative.
- **Special parameters:** interior knots, at continuity p − multiplicity.

### 3.7 Generic algorithms (`curves/approx.h`)

All three algorithms first split the range at the special parameters.

**Box bound.** `bounds(view, t0, t1)`: on each sub-range, the endpoints' box widened
by:
- h²/8 · √D · deriv_bound(2), the error of linear interpolation;
- `eval_error`;
- rounding, applied outward.

**Flattening.** `flatten(view, t0, t1, tol)` returns sorted parameters whose polyline
lies within tol of the curve.
- Starting from the special parameters, bisect recursively until
  h²/8 · √D · M₂ + eval_error ≤ tol.
- Bisection from the same root partition makes the parameter set for tol/4 a superset
  of the one for tol, so LODs nest.

**Approximation.** `approximate(store, ref, t0, t1, tol)` interns a degree-5 `bspline`
cache within `max_error` ≤ tol of the exact curve. It is parameterised by the source
parameter, and the source is an inner ref.
- **Pieces.** Hermite data (r, r', r'') are taken at both ends of each piece, one-sided
  at special parameters (`Side`).
- **Error bound per piece:**
  - h⁶/(2⁶·6!) · √D · deriv_bound(6), the interpolation bound;
  - plus Λ₀·eval_error + Λ₁·h·deriv_eval_error(1) + Λ₂·h²·deriv_eval_error(2). Λ_j
    are the sup-norms of the quintic Hermite basis functions on [0, 1], constants
    computed once and rounded up;
  - plus the rounding of the Bézier conversion.
- **Bisect** while the bound exceeds tol.
- **Knot multiplicity** at a join is 5 − min(continuity, 2):
  - 3 where the source is C2 or smoother;
  - 4 at C1 (a κ jump);
  - 5 at C0 (a polyline vertex).
- **Arc length** is exact for `ArcLength` kinds. A general certified arc length is
  P4b.

**Approximation caches are rooted by their holder.** `ApproxCache` is a weak map from
(source ref, tol) to the B-spline ref.
- It is a `RefRoot` with `prune`, as 07 §13 allows.
- It is checked against `PoolStore::version()`.
- The editor does not keep approximations in P4a.

### 3.8 The oracle (`tools/curve_oracle/`)

- **A GCC-only tool,** using `__float128` and libquadmath, built in WSL. It is never
  linked into the core and never in ctest (DEC-002 permits offline oracles).
- **Method:** independent of production. It runs composite Gauss–Legendre directly on
  e^{iθ(s)}, with nodes computed in quad precision.
  - It uses two panel counts, which must agree to 10⁻²⁸.
  - It evaluates θ, w, κ, cosh and sinh in quad.
- **What it covers:** the §6 curves, and the `detmath` functions over their ranges
  (for the ulp bounds).
- **Output:** `tests/data/curve_oracle.inc`, as hex doubles. The file is checked in,
  so the tests run the same in every configuration. The tool is rerun by hand when
  the fixture set changes.

## 4. The geometry store in `Complex`

### 4.1 Store and columns

- **The store.** `Complex` gains `attr::PoolStore geom`. Kinds are registered in the
  constructor in this order: `cesaro2` = 1, `offset2` = 2, `alignment3` = 3,
  `catenary3` = 4, `polyline3` = 5, `bspline` = 6. New kinds are appended.
- **`topo::GeomRef`** becomes an alias of `attr::PoolRef`, and `Geom<Name>` columns
  become `attr::Column<attr::PoolRef>`. The old 4-byte type is renamed
  `LegacyGeomRef` and kept, with its `ValueKey`, for `io_legacy.cc` only.
- **New columns** (all Topo):
  - `.e.range`: `Column<math::double2>`, default (0, 0);
  - `.e.geom_flags`: `Flags8`, bit 0 = "pcurve primary" (05 §1). It must be 0 in
    P4a; adding it now avoids a format 4 in P4b;
  - `.g.curve_tol`: `Column<double>`, default 2⁻³⁰. Topo, so `set_attr` cannot write
    it, and P4a has no editor primitive for it.
- **Copy and move stay defaulted** (07 §13).
- **`frozen()`** also requires the store's version to equal the one recorded at
  freeze, so interning after a freeze clears frozen. `version()` itself ignores the
  store, so interning through the editor is not an outside write.

### 4.2 Roots and accepted kinds

- **Roots:** all domain groups in schema order, plus `global`. The editor adds
  `JournalRoots`, which covers row captures, barrier snapshots, `SetAttr` bytes and
  the op payloads that hold refs (§5.2).
- **`Roots::accept`:**
  - `.e.curve` accepts the 3D kinds only: `alignment3`, `catenary3`, `polyline3`, and
    `bspline` with D = 3;
  - `.c.pcurve` and `.f.surface` accept nothing in P4a;
  - inner refs: `offset2.base` must be a `cesaro2`, `alignment3.horizontal` a
    `cesaro2` or `offset2`, and a `bspline.source` any curve kind.

### 4.3 Hashes, freeze and I/O

**litestl addition:** `PoolStore::canonical_map(roots)` computes the phase-1
first-reference order without mutating anything. The cost is O(ref rows) plus
O(entries in the closure); columns whose pages are all Constant are skipped page by
page. With it:
- `hash_into(roots, map)` hashes the closure in canonical order;
- `AttrGroup::hash_into(h, ref_map)` hashes ref columns translated through the map.

**`content_hash` and `topology_hash`:**
- They hash the groups through the canonical map. `content_hash` also includes the
  canonical store hash.
- There is one path. An empty closure gives an empty map, and the store term is the
  hash of an empty closure.
- The cost is O(ref rows) on top of today's: no copy and no renumbering.

**`freeze`** canonicalises the live store after its permutes, with the domain groups
as roots (07 §13). It is an outside write, so the editor's journal goes stale.

**`compact` and `rebuild_regions`** leave the store's numbering alone.

**`write` (format 3):**
1. header and floors;
2. the canonical store;
3. the groups.

It writes from a canonicalised copy (07 §13). Write is not on a hot path.

**`read`, format 3**, follows the strict owner sequence:
1. a fresh complex;
2. `store.read` — each kind's `read` re-canonicalises its entry, compares it bitwise
   with what it read, refuses non-finite values and caps the piece count (§3.2);
3. the group reads;
4. `audit(require_reachable)`;
5. `canonicalize`, which must report no change;
6. move-assign.

**`read`, formats 1 and 2** (`io_legacy.cc`):
- Legacy schemas hold the old column layouts: the 4-byte `"gref"` `Geom` columns, and
  no `.e.range`, `.e.geom_flags` or `.g.curve_tol`.
- A legacy hash checks the stream's trailing hash.
- The data is converted into a fresh format-3 complex: every legacy `Geom` value must
  be NONE, and the new columns take their defaults.
- The converted complex must validate exactly as it did before.
- **Fixtures:** at the start of W5, before the switch, the current code writes format-2
  and format-1 streams of the `test_topo_freeze` city (`tests/data/utcx_f1.bin`,
  `utcx_f2.bin`) with their content and topology hashes, plus a structure hash that
  excludes geometry columns. The legacy test reads them and checks that structure hash
  after conversion.

## 5. Editing

### 5.1 Edge carrier semantics

- **Meaning.** `.e.curve` = ref and `.e.range` = (t₀, t₁) mean that vertex `vs[0]` is
  at carrier(t₀) and `vs[1]` at carrier(t₁).
  - t₀ > t₁ is allowed: the edge runs against the carrier's direction.
  - The two ends must differ: t₀ ≠ t₁.
  - The range may not cross a fold of an `offset2` (`offset_valid`).
- **NONE** means a straight segment, with range (0, 0).
- **Sharing.** Many edges may share one carrier with different ranges.
- **Equality of carriers is by vertex.** Two edges carry the same carrier between the
  same vertices when the refs are equal and the ranges agree after swapping (t₀, t₁)
  for whichever edge has its `vs` reversed.

### 5.2 Primitives and barriers

- **`intern_curve(def) → PoolRef`** canonicalises, validates and interns. It is not
  journaled, and it does not change `version()`.
- **`set_edge_curve(e, ref, range)`:**
  - **Checks:** e is live; ref is NONE or live and of an accepted kind; the range lies
    in the domain.
  - **Effects:** writes both columns through `touch`. The endpoint check is left to
    validation (§5.3).
  - **Journal:** `OpKind::SetEdgeCurve`, u = [e, pool, slot, t₀ lo, t₀ hi, t₁ lo,
    t₁ hi]. Doubles take two words.
  - `JournalRoots` visits the payload's ref.
- **`split_edge_at(e, t, &v, &e2)`**, `OpKind::SplitEdgeAt` with t in two words:
  - t must lie strictly inside the range.
  - The new vertex goes at `round_C(carrier(t))`. The children take (t₀, t) and
    (t, t₁) and share the ref.
  - **The chord skeleton changes**, so it follows `set_position`'s rules:
    - every incident planar face must stay planar (a non-planar result fails
      `NonPlanar`);
    - projected faces re-derive their radial order;
    - `regions_dirty` is set.
  - IDs and slots are as in `split_edge`. Propagation weights use the parameter
    fraction (t − t₀)/(t₁ − t₀), not the chord fraction.
- **`split_edge(e, p)`** on a curved edge is refused with `CurvedEdge`.
- **`join_edge(v)`** on curved edges requires:
  - the same ref, with ranges that meet at v's parameter and continue in the same
    direction;
  - planarity and the projected-face rules, because the corner disappears from the
    skeleton;
  - and it sets `regions_dirty`.

  The joined range is the union. Otherwise it fails with `Incompatible`. Propagation
  uses the parameter fraction. The collinearity test applies to straight edges only.
- **`weld`** fuses parallel edges only if both are straight, or both carry the same
  carrier between the same vertices (§5.1). Otherwise it fails with `Incompatible`:
  P4a has no curved digons (OI-P4a-1).
- **No other primitive changes.**
  - `imprint`, `unimprint`, `split_face`, `add_hole` and `remove_hole` keep
    `.e.curve` and `.e.range`.
  - Physical undo restores them from row captures.
- **Barrier undo and rollback keep the live store.** Today they do
  `k_ = std::move(*snapshot)` (`editor_impl_txn.h:246, :344`). That becomes: move the
  live store out, assign the snapshot, move the live store back.
  - This is sound because the editor never renumbers the live store, and
    `JournalRoots` roots every ref in a snapshot, so the snapshot's refs are still
    live.
  - Barriers collect only after they commit, when the snapshot is already in the
    journal.
- **`collect_geometry()`:**
  - Refused inside a transaction (`OpenTransaction`): uncommitted row captures are not
    roots.
  - It first runs the stale-stamp check, which drops a journal invalidated by an
    outside write such as freeze.
  - It then collects with the complex groups and `JournalRoots` as roots.

### 5.3 Validation

- **New codes** in the embedding layer:
  - `CurveKind`: the ref is not live, or its kind is not accepted;
  - `CurveRange`: the range lies outside the domain, the ends are equal, the range
    crosses a fold, or a NONE ref has a non-zero range;
  - `CurveEndpoint`: an end vertex lies further than the §2 tolerance from the carrier.
- **`GeometryUnsupported`:** a non-NONE `.c.pcurve` or `.f.surface`, or a non-zero
  `.e.geom_flags`.
- **Local validation** checks the listed edges. The touched-row filter lists every
  edge whose vertices or columns changed. `.g.curve_tol` cannot change inside the
  editor.
- **The skeleton check is unchanged:** faces and wires are validated on their chords
  (OI-P4a-1).

### 5.4 Goldens

- **Why every hash changes:**
  - the `Geom` columns widen from 4 to 8 bytes;
  - the new columns are added;
  - the random step mix gains curve steps.
- **Proof that nothing else moved the hashes** (W5): the structure hash that excludes
  geometry columns, recorded with the fixtures before the switch, must be equal after
  it, for:
  - the P2 determinism scenario;
  - `test_edit_random` seeds 1–3, run without curve steps.

  The helper is a temporary `CLAUDENOTE:` test hook.
- **Re-pinned twice:** once in W5 (the type change), and again in W6 (the curve

## 11. Implementation notes (changes made while building)

**W2 (`cesaro2`):**
- Pieces are accepted on the error terms that bisection reduces faster than h
  (Taylor tail, coefficient uncertainty, recurrence rounding). The terms proportional
  to h (heading uncertainty, Horner and rotation rounding) stay in the certified bound
  but no longer drive bisection, which otherwise never terminated (`debugging.md`).
- The 10 km chain is certified to 2.5e-10 m. Its measured error against the oracle is
  about 1% of that.

**W3 (`bspline`, `approx`):**
- **Continuity semantics.** `SpecialParam::continuity` is the true order (C^k and no
  smoother). `kSmooth` (255) now means C-infinity: a feature such as an inflection or a
  curvature extremum, not a break. `cesaro2` joins compare κ, κ′, κ″ and κ‴, and a
  `bspline` knot reports p − m. The generic algorithms cut only at breaks.
  - The old "C3 or better" meaning hid a quintic's simple knots (C4) from the
    approximation, although r⁽⁶⁾ is unbounded across them.
  - It also cut at root clusters just inside a segment end, which left sliver pieces.
- **Exact-data joins.** A `cesaro2` join whose κ values differ by rounding in the
  definition, such as (k/L)·L against k, really is C1. The approximation then puts a
  multiplicity-4 knot there. This is correct and costs one control point.
- **Interpolation bounds have no √D factor.** The Peano kernels of linear and of
  Hermite interpolation have one sign, so the bounds h²/8·M₂ and h⁶/(2⁶·6!)·M₆ hold
  for the Euclidean norm (`approx.h`).
- **`bspline` deriv_bound** uses the convex hull of the order-k derivative control
  points over the spans in range, not per-span Bézier conversion. The bound is just as
  certified, and simpler.
- **Knot multiplicity** 5 − min(continuity, 2) is reached by assembling the spline
  from the Hermite Bézier pieces through blossoms (`detail::assemble`).
  - A control point shared by several pieces is the mean of their blossoms, and the
    distance it moves each piece is added to `max_error`.
  - Where that doesn't fit tol, the join falls back to multiplicity 5.
  - Half of tol is reserved for this.
- **`ApproxCache` liveness** needs ephemerons, which litestl now has
  (`RefRoot::visit_weak_pairs`, iterated to a fixpoint in collect, audit and
  canonicalize; canonicalize now also calls `prune`).
  - A cached spline names its source as an inner ref. Held strongly, it would keep its
    own source alive; held as a plain weak ref, it would die at every collect.
  - A lookup checks the cached spline's content (source, range, max_error ≤ tol)
    instead of `PoolStore::version()`. Version changes on every intern miss, so it
    can't tell a stale ref from a valid one; the content check can.
- **Pool ids.** `bspline` is registered second in `kinds()` until W4 adds the four
  kinds that go before it (CLAUDENOTE in `curve.cc`).

**W4 (`offset2`, `alignment3`, `catenary3`, `polyline3`):**
- **`offset2` continuity.** At a base join of continuity C^k, the offset is C^(k−1).
  A κ jump changes |r_w′| = |1 − wκ|, so a lane edge over a C1 clothoid–arc join is
  only C0. The approximation of such an edge gets multiplicity-5 knots there.
- **`offset2` derivatives** are the polynomials Q_j, built per span where w and κ are
  both single polynomials. The rounding of the spans' local origins is charged to
  `deriv_eval_error`.
- **`offset2` and `alignment3`** validate their inner refs against the store in
  `make` and again when the view is built (`dim`/`make2` report 0 or null for an entry
  whose base is gone or wrong). A stream read can't check refs, because `read` has no
  store; W5's `Roots::accept` and validation codes cover that.
- **`w` and `z` must be continuous.** `make` refuses a provable jump at a break, and
  the remaining rounding-level jump (`Fn1D::max_jump`) is added to `eval_error`.
- **`catenary3`:**
  - `from_sag` uses 120 bisection steps on log a, not Newton. It is as deterministic
    and doesn't need a derivative.
  - The sag is measured vertically from the chord's midpoint.
  - The tests' reference is the closed form in long double (libm `sinhl`/`asinhl`),
    which is independent of `detmath`, rather than the quad oracle tool.
  - The rounding of the derived c is charged through ∂g/∂c ≤ 2·sinh(Y).
- **`polyline3`** refuses repeated consecutive points, since a zero-length span has no
  tangent.
- **`bspline`** gains `insert_knot` (Boehm) for the shape test. The result is a
  primary spline with no source.
- **`kinds()`** is in the plan's order now: `cesaro2` = 1 … `bspline` = 6.

**W5 (the store in `Complex`, format 3, legacy reading):**
- **Fixtures are `.inc` byte arrays** (`tests/data/utcx_legacy.inc`), not `.bin`
  files. This avoids plumbing a data path into the tests.
  - The file holds four streams: formats 1 and 2, in double and in Lattice.
  - Each comes with its old content and topology hashes, its structure hash and its
    validation issue counts.
  - The format-2 streams carry non-zero face and edge floors.
  - The city has 3 blocks, which keeps the file at 540 KB.
- **The structure hash hashes the kept columns itself.** AttrGroup's filtered hash
  also covers the group's builtin count, which the new columns change.
  - The first pinning used the filtered hash and broke at the switch (`debugging.md`).
  - Values were recomputed with the format-2 code before the switch landed.
- **The goldens proof.** The structure goldens (test_edit_random, test_topo_determinism)
  were pinned with the format-2 code and are unchanged by W5. The content and topology
  goldens were then re-pinned.
- **Legacy conversion** uses the new litestl `AttrGroup::migrate_from`. It moves
  columns by name and shares them copy-on-write, so dynamic columns survive and the new
  builtins take their defaults.
- **`read` (format 3)** runs `audit(require_reachable)` before `canonicalize`, even
  though canonicalize's own validation would also refuse a dangling ref. The audit's
  value is the diagnostic, and the test pins its message.
- **`freeze`** audits the store before any permute, returning the new
  `FreezeResult::GeometryInvalid` with nothing changed. Its canonicalisation after the
  permutes then cannot fail.
- **`write`** aborts on a store that doesn't validate, because write returns void.
  Every editor path validates refs before committing (W6).
- **Accept checks read only the kind** (and a bspline's dimension) and build no
  evaluator, so collect and hashing stay O(ref rows).

**W6 (the editor):**
- **`intern_curve(kind, def, &ref)`** is a template over the kind's value type.
  - It validates through the kind's own write/read round trip: every kind's `read`
    re-runs `make` (directly or through `Fn1D::read`) and refuses non-canonical
    definitions.
  - The checks that need the store (an alignment's z inside its horizontal, an offset's
    base) run when the evaluator is built, so `intern_curve` requires the evaluator to
    build. A refused definition can leave an unreferenced entry until the next collect,
    which never changes a hash.
  - An extra `equal(def)` check was dropped as redundant: its mutant survived.
- **Shared code.** `split_edge` and `split_edge_at` share `split_impl`. The curved path
  splits the ranges and uses the parameter fraction for propagation.
- **Checks outside the editor.** `edge_carrier` and `on_carrier` (`topo/curve_geom.h`)
  serve both the editor and `validate_curves.cc`. The validator runs the curve checks at
  the start of the embedding layer.
- **`JournalRoots`** visits `SetEdgeCurve` payload refs. Barrier undo and rollback go
  through `restore_snapshot`, which keeps the live store. `run_barrier` collects after it
  commits.
- **`test_edit_random`'s double-vs-Lattice check** now compares `structure_hash(k,
  coord_free)`, which leaves out positions and geometry columns. Carriers are in frame
  units, which differ by the 2⁸ grid scale between the two types.
  - The runs may differ step by step: Lattice's half-quantum endpoint tolerance accepts
    a `set_position` near a curved edge's end that double refuses (seed 3).
  - The final structures agree.
- **Goldens re-pinned (the second time, §5.4):** content and structure, because the curve
  steps change the random sequence. The W5 values are kept in the comment.
- **Mutation check:** 20 mutants, all killed, after one test was strengthened.
